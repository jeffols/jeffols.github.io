Point of view

How I think

Ontology as architecture

Systems inherit the shape of the concepts they model. Get the ontology right and the code writes itself. Get it wrong and no amount of engineering saves you.

Context over instruction

The best systems don't need more rules. They need better context. Engineer the information environment and let intelligence emerge from structure.

Simplicity is the residue of understanding

Complexity is not a problem to manage. It is a symptom of incomplete comprehension. Simplify by understanding more deeply, not by hiding more aggressively.

Speed comes from anticipation

The only way to move fast is to know what every team will ask before they ask it. Security, data, infrastructure, architecture, the C-suite. Their questions are design constraints, not gates to clear later. Anticipate them and you stop trading one pillar against another.

Writing

Working Faster is about where delivery time actually goes. Published essays and working notes, newest first.

Agent Engineering, a Reading Plan

Four weeks through prompt, loop, graph, and the knowledge representation layer underneath all three. Sixty five readings across six phases, each one annotated for what it actually gives you and where it belongs in the sequence.

You will learn why the agent memory field keeps rediscovering ontology engineering, and what putting one non probabilistic node on a critical path buys you that a second model never will.

"On Track" Is Not a State

"On track" is a claim about the date. It is not a description of the work. Twenty minutes of real effort and a week in someone else's queue sound exactly the same, and the calendar moves either way.

You will learn how to hear the difference between a status and a state, and the one extra question that gives a wait an owner and a next signal.

Complaints Don't Travel Upward. Costs Do.

A complaint names a feeling, and a feeling can only be sympathized with or dismissed. A cost names a wait, a size, and an ask, in the terms the person deciding already tracks.

You will learn how to turn a recurring friction you do not own into a number someone can act on, and why keeping the friction can still be the right answer.
